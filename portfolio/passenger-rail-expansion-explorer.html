Passenger Rail Expansion Explorer

One of my big passions is transportation. Like many Americans who grew up in the shadow of Amtrak, I have not ridden many trains for transportation in my lifetime. Only when I visited Europe did I experience the joys of train travel. This inspired me to advocate for expanded passenger rail service on this continent. The project in question is the culmination of a year's work of web app building in ArcGIS Online. A two-part question I asked in creating this project is, to what kind of places can trains take us and, what will the economic impact of passenger rail have on our state? … I knew where to look for inspiration. Organizations and advocacy groups such as Amtrak’s 2035 initiative and the High Speed Rail Alliance (HSRA) put out a series of static maps that I could convert into dynamic web apps for people to visualize as a more complete picture of transit. With my web map created, and the layers I sought to display added, I then created two spinoff Sidebar web apps.


The first map visualizes passenger rail expansion in Wisconsin. The various features here include both currently operating and proposed passenger rail lines such as the Borealis, and the proposed Hiawatha West lines, respectively. Other features include economic impact zones (as denoted by the big circles), destinations, such as Green Bay Packers football, Wisconsin Badgers football & basketball, Summerfest, the Blue Ox fest, and more. Additionally, there's a network of bus routes for connecting people to cities without passenger rail service.


The second has some of the same features as the first but focuses more on the nation at large. The main layers here are the proposed and currently existing Amtrak routes, and a network of proposed and active passenger rail stops. Although there is a distinction between the two, integrating proposed and current stops better conveys how a future train network would function, and could get more would-be riders on board (no pun intended).
